01 Metrology Development

Creating measurement capability for previously unquantifiable product characteristics.

The Challenge

Due to the unique nature of the product, existing inspection methods could not reliably quantify certain surface and optical material characteristics. New metrology methods were therefore needed to translate previously subjective or unmeasurable quality characteristics into objective, repeatable measurements

My Approach

I led R&D and deployment of metrology systems for those unverifiable dimensions, taking concepts from early development through full-machine demonstrations. This involved working with vendors and integrators to develop the equipment, and then testing and validating the equipment with representative parts.

03 Equipment & Quality Systems

Delivering measurement and quality systems for production.

Inspection Equipment Retrofit

Challenge

Two existing production inspection systems were not reliably reproducing measurements obtained through laboratory inspection, creating a gap between production-line measurement and the established reference method. The systems needed to be improved without replacing the existing inspection platforms.

Approach

I investigated the measurement gaps and worked with the original equipment vendor to define the required retrofit. I translated the measurement needs into equipment requirements, reviewed proposed design changes, and coordinated technical decisions between the vendor and internal engineering teams through development and implementation.

For both machines I took on a project management role where I drove the retrofit progress. Checking on vendor progress, coordinating with internal engineering teams, and ensuring the retrofit was on track to meet the schedule.

Outcome

I drove the retrofit from requirements through implementation, delivering the updated inspection systems on schedule with the measurement capabilities required for production.

Production Quality Control Planning

Challenge

A new automated production line was being developed without an established quality-control strategy. Inspection and control requirements needed to be defined across incoming material, the manufacturing process, and the finished product before the line entered production.

Approach

I worked cross-functionally with the teams developing the new line to understand the manufacturing flow, identify critical quality requirements, and determine where controls were needed throughout the process.

I evaluated the flow from incoming material through in-process operations and final inspection, defining how quality would be monitored and where issues needed to be detected before progressing downstream.

I then consolidated these requirements into a production quality-control plan, aligning inspection points, quality requirements, and ownership with the teams responsible for implementing the line.

Outcome

I established the quality-control framework for the new production line, spanning incoming material, in-process manufacturing, and final product quality so that the required controls could be incorporated before launch.

04 Key Takeaways

Measure Before You Specify

Developing new metrology reinforced how closely measurement capability and product requirements are connected. A specification is only useful if the characteristic can be measured repeatably and objectively.

Design Intent Has to Meet Manufacturing Reality

Dimensional convergence taught me that an out-of-spec part does not automatically mean the manufacturing process is wrong. Solving the problem requires understanding design intent, process capability, measurement strategy, and how they interact.

Quality Starts Before Inspection

Working on a new automated line reinforced that quality cannot simply be added at the end of a process. Effective controls need to be designed into the manufacturing flow from incoming material through the finished product.
