01 Rapid-Deploy Cobot Inspection

Developing a flexible inspection concept for fast response to unexpected quality issues.

The Challenge

Quality spills require teams to quickly understand why parts are failing, but traditional containment can depend on bringing in additional personnel, training them on the inspection method, and manually collecting defect data.

The team wanted a faster and more repeatable inspection approach that could be deployed quickly when unexpected quality issues appeared.

My Approach

I researched commercially available cobot inspection concepts to understand what could be adapted rather than developed entirely from scratch.

I evaluated how an off-the-shelf cobot station could be modified with reusable fixturing and internal software to support a wider range of parts and quality-spill scenarios.

The concept centered on simple operator setup: an operator could use drag-and-drop software to define where and what the cobot should inspect, then load and unload parts while the cobot handled the inspection sequence and data collection.

R&D Focus

Deployability

Could the station be moved and brought online quickly for an unexpected quality spill?

Flexibility

Could robust, reusable fixturing support different part types and inspection needs?

Usability

Could an operator configure the inspection without specialized robot-programming expertise?

Outcome

Developed a rapid-deployment cobot inspection concept that reduced setup time by 40% while shifting repetitive inspection and data collection away from manual operators.

02 Materials Testing & Characterization

Turning raw material samples into usable mechanical-property data for R&D teams.

The Purpose

R&D programs were evaluating different steel and aluminum material configurations, requiring consistent mechanical test data to understand how different material recipes and process conditions were performing.

My Role

I supported these programs by preparing and testing more than 2,000 samples, generating mechanical-property data that engineers could use to compare material configurations and understand expected part behavior.

My work covered the full testing workflow, from extracting and preparing specimens from production parts through mechanical testing, microscopy, and results reporting. This allowed R&D engineers to receive consistent test data without having to perform each test themselves, helping experiments and material investigations continue moving forward.

  1. Prepare Extracted and prepared representative specimens using waterjet cutting, polishing, and tumbling.
  2. Test Performed tensile testing, hardness testing, and weld-section evaluation.
  3. Analyze Examined mechanical behavior and compared results across material configurations.
  4. Report Provided consistent test results to R&D engineers for ongoing material and product development.

Testing & Preparation

Tensile Testing · Hardness Testing · Weld Etching · Microscopy · Waterjet Cutting · Polishing · Tumbling

Outcome

Prepared, tested, and analyzed 2,000+ steel and aluminum samples, providing R&D teams with consistent mechanical-property data to support material evaluation and ongoing product development.

03 Key Takeaways

Design for Fast Deployment

The cobot project reinforced that useful automation is not only about capability. For quality-response applications, setup time, portability, and ease of use can be just as important as the inspection itself.

Automation Should Reduce Operator Complexity

Designing around simple drag-and-drop setup showed me the value of making technical systems accessible to the people who actually need to use them, rather than requiring specialized expertise for every deployment.

Good R&D Depends on Consistent Data

Materials testing showed me how valuable reliable test data is to engineering development. Consistent preparation, testing, and reporting lets engineers compare ideas and keep experiments moving forward.
