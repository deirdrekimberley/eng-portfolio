01 Process Risk & PFMEA

Identifying and mitigating manufacturing risks for a new wheel-alignment process.

The Challenge

Cybercab introduced a new wheel-alignment process as part of Tesla's unboxed manufacturing approach. With a new process came new failure modes and limited historical production data, making it important to identify potential risks before they could affect production.

My Approach

I began by reviewing wheel-alignment processes from previous vehicle programs and carrying forward relevant lessons learned. I then worked with process engineering to break down the new Cybercab alignment process step-by-step, identifying where failures could occur, what could cause them, and how they could be detected.

I documented these risks through PFMEA documentation, evaluating severity, occurrence, existing detection methods, and overall risk priority. For higher-priority risks, I worked with the process team to identify opportunities to eliminate the failure mode, reduce its likelihood, or introduce additional detection controls.

  1. Process Reviewed the manufacturing process step-by-step to understand where risk could enter the system.
  2. Failure Identified potential failure modes associated with each process step.
  3. Cause Root-caused where failures could originate to better understand how they could be prevented.
  4. Assess Evaluated severity, likelihood of occurrence, detection capability, and overall risk priority.
  5. Control Worked with process engineering to eliminate risks where possible or add controls that could detect them earlier.
  6. Monitor A PFMEA is a living document that is updated as the process evolves, risk priority is reassessed as the process changes, and controls are added or removed as needed.

Outcome

Developed the initial PFMEA for the new wheel-alignment process, documenting 150+ process-risk lines and identifying 100+ potential risks. The analysis established a baseline risk framework for the process and drove mitigation, additional detection controls, and ongoing monitoring before production maturity.

02 Rapid Machine Vision Development

Evaluating deployable vision systems for quality inspection on early-build manufacturing lines.

The Challenge

Existing automated vision systems were better suited to mature production lines, where additional development time and specialized expertise could be supported. Early-build lines needed a faster and more accessible way to introduce automated quality checks while products and processes were still evolving.

My Approach

I evaluated off-the-shelf vision hardware and machine-learning software as a potential solution for rapid quality inspection. I learned the equipment and vision software, developed representative inspection setups, and tested the system across different parts, defects, and operating conditions to understand where it could reliably support early-build use cases.

Rather than only demonstrating successful inspections, I characterized the system's limitations, documenting where detection performance broke down, what setup conditions affected results, and which applications were appropriate for the technology.

R&D Focus

Detection

What defects and features could the system reliably identify?

Robustness

How did performance change across parts, setups, and operating conditions?

Usability

Could engineers quickly configure and deploy inspections without specialized vision expertise?

  1. Evaluate Learned the vision hardware and machine-learning software and assessed its fit for early-build quality inspection.
  2. Test Created representative parts, defects, and environments to evaluate real-world inspection use cases.
  3. Characterize Documented where the system performed reliably and where its technical limitations appeared.
  4. Document Consolidated test data, capabilities, limitations, and setup guidance into internal documentation.
  5. Transfer Shared findings with other teams and trained engineers on how to configure and use the equipment.

Deployment & Knowledge Transfer

I consolidated the test results into internal documentation defining system capabilities, limitations, setup guidance, and validated use cases. I shared the findings with other teams and trained engineers on how to configure and use the equipment for rapid inspection applications.

Outcome

Established validated use cases for rapid machine-vision inspection, documenting system capabilities, limitations, and setup requirements while enabling other teams to evaluate and adopt the technology for early-build quality checks.

03 Traceability Process Redesign

Redesigning part-traceability processes to surface launch risks before they reached production.

Vehicle traceability links the exact parts installed in each vehicle to their manufacturing and supplier history, supporting production tracking, quality investigation, and targeted response when issues occur.

The Challenge

Lessons learned from previous launches highlighted gaps in how traceability requirements were defined, communicated, and verified across teams. These gaps could allow issues to progress downstream before being detected, increasing launch risk.

My Approach

I reviewed lessons learned from previous vehicle programs and mapped the existing traceability workflow to understand where requirements, ownership, and communication were breaking down.

I worked cross-functionally with material planning, supplier industrialization, design, manufacturing, and other key stakeholders to understand how each team interacted with the process and where expectations differed.

From that analysis, I developed a standardized traceability process with clearer requirements, responsibilities, and checkpoints designed to catch issues earlier in the product-release and supplier workflow.

I documented failures observed in previous programs and compared them against issues surfaced by the redesigned process, building evidence for how the new controls could prevent similar problems from progressing into launch.

Process Redesign Principles

Standardize

Define clearer traceability requirements and procedures before they move downstream.

Align

Create shared expectations and ownership across the teams involved in the process.

Verify

Introduce checkpoints to surface gaps earlier before they reach production.

Outcome

Redesigned the traceability process around clearer standards, cross-functional alignment, and earlier verification, surfacing 200+ issues that could be addressed before progressing further toward launch.

The resulting process proposal and supporting analysis were handed off to the team for leadership review following my internship.

04 Key Takeaways

Design Out Risk Early

Developing a PFMEA for a new manufacturing process taught me that manufacturing quality is as much about anticipating failures as reacting to them. Breaking down a process before production can expose risks early enough to eliminate them or build in the controls needed to detect them.

Validate the Limits, Not Just the Successes

Machine-vision R&D reinforced that proving a technology works is only part of validation. Understanding where it fails, what conditions affect performance, and where it should not be used is just as important when deciding whether a technology is ready for manufacturing.

Good Processes Need Clear Standards

The traceability project showed me how gaps in requirements, ownership, and communication between teams can become physical production problems downstream. Clear standards and early checkpoints can surface those gaps before they become launch issues.
